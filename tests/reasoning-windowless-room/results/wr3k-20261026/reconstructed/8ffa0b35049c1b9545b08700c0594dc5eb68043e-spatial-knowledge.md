# Spatial Knowledge — Rooms, Walls, Ceilings, and Floors

## Indoor Spaces

A room is an enclosed space within a building or structure. Rooms are defined by their boundaries: walls (vertical surfaces), a floor (the bottom surface), and a ceiling (the top surface). Together, these form the room's envelope.

## Windowless Rooms

A windowless room is a room that has no windows. Windows are openings in walls that allow natural light to enter from outside. In a windowless room:
- No natural light can enter from outside the room
- The only light sources are artificial (e.g., light bulbs, lamps)
- The room is completely enclosed on all sides

## Ceilings

A ceiling is the overhead surface of a room. It forms the upper boundary of the enclosed space. When you are inside a room and look upward, you see the ceiling.

Key properties of ceilings:
- They are horizontal surfaces above the occupant's head
- They are typically flat or slightly angled
- They are part of the room's structure
- They separate the room from the space above (another room, an attic, or the roof)

## Attics

An attic is a space directly below the roof of a building. When the prompt says "windowless room / attic," it means the room being described is an attic or attic-like space. Attics typically have:
- Sloped ceilings (following the roof line)
- Limited or no windows
- Dark interior (no natural light penetration without artificial lighting)

## Floors

A floor is the bottom surface of a room. When you stand in a room, you stand on the floor. The floor is opposite the ceiling.

## Walls

Walls are the vertical surfaces that enclose a room. They connect the floor to the ceiling. In a windowless room, walls have no openings for windows.

## The Envelope

The room's envelope (walls + floor + ceiling) completely separates the interior from the exterior. Nothing outside the room's envelope is visible from inside the room — no sky, no sun, no stars, no external objects.

## Looking Up Indoors

When a person inside a room looks upward, their line of sight travels from their eyes to the nearest surface above them. In a room, that surface is the ceiling. The line of sight cannot pass through the ceiling to see the sky above because the ceiling is opaque and blocks the view.

## Light Bulbs and Lamps

Light bulbs and lamps are artificial light sources that can be mounted on ceilings (in the case of bulbs) or placed on floors/furniture (in the case of lamps). When you "turn on a bulb/lamp" in a room, the light source illuminates the room's interior. The bulb itself is typically attached to the ceiling (in the case of a ceiling-mounted light fixture).

## Summary of Key Facts

- A room is enclosed by walls, floor, and ceiling
- A windowless room has no openings for natural light
- When inside a room and looking up, you see the ceiling
- The ceiling is opaque — you cannot see through it
- Light bulbs are typically mounted on ceilings
- When you turn on a light in a dark room, the room becomes illuminated
- The surface you see when looking up in a room is the ceiling
